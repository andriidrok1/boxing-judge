# Draft post (X / LinkedIn). Andrii edits, English. Numbers from out/scores.csv, 2026-09-18 run.

I built a small system that scores boxing rounds from broadcast video and tested it on Fury vs Usyk 1.

Pipeline: YOLO11-pose on every frame, camera-cut detection so tracks don't bleed across replays, CLIP zero-shot to tell the fighters from the referee (a text prompt like "referee in a white shirt", no training), trunks colour to tell Fury from Usyk, then hand-written judging logic: punches thrown, ring control, aggression, 10-point must.

Result: 116-111 Usyk. The judges had it 115-112 Usyk, 114-113 Usyk, 114-113 Fury. Same winner, one round wider than the widest card, and it agrees with each judge on 8 to 9 of the 12 rounds.

Where it is wrong is more interesting than where it is right. Rounds 3 and 4 are where it splits from the judges, and those are exactly the rounds where the judges split from each other. Round 12 it gives to Usyk on volume; all three judges gave it to Fury. CompuBox says Fury landed more in those rounds while throwing about the same. My system counts punches thrown, it cannot see landed, so anything Fury won on accuracy looks like a coin flip to it. That is the whole limitation in one sentence, and it is why I did not ship a landed/blocked detector: at 25 fps a jab is three frames of motion blur, and I would rather report "thrown" honestly than "landed" badly.

Three bugs worth sharing. The referee. Under the red Riyadh lighting his white shirt has the same HSV as skin, so every colour heuristic I wrote called him a fighter. ByteTrack made it worse by dropping Fury for a frame whenever he moved fast, so the ref inherited his track. I ended up removing the tracker entirely and linking detections myself (two objects per frame is not a hard tracking problem), and letting CLIP reject the ref on close-ups where it is confident. On wide shots CLIP is useless (a 100-pixel crop is "a spectator" to it), so there the trunks brightness decides.

The second one was my own rule: "if two boxes in a frame get the same name, flip the weaker one". YOLO sometimes draws two boxes on one man, and the rule turned the second box into a phantom opponent throwing phantom punches. Now a box that overlaps another by more than 30% is dropped, not renamed.

The third: a camera cut looks exactly like a punch to a velocity detector, because the wrist jumps across the frame. I split every time series at cuts and ignore the first frames after one.

And one that the frame-by-frame check found: absolute colour rules break when the lighting changes between shots. "Dark trunks = Fury" was wrong for a whole 6-second stretch where Usyk stood in shadow. What works is relative: of the two people in the frame, the one with darker trunks and greener gloves is Fury. Same information, but compared inside the frame instead of against a fixed number.

The one that hurt the demo most: after the knockdown the camera holds on Fury's face and chest for six seconds, and CLIP called that crop "referee" the whole time. The fix is embarrassing in hindsight: a referee wears a shirt, so a chain with a bare torso can never be the referee, whatever CLIP says.

And the one that made the fighters swap names in the demo clip: in a clinch YOLO's box for Fury widens until it reaches where Usyk's track was a few frames ago, the linker attaches it to Usyk's track, and the real Usyk box gets Fury's. Position alone cannot tell them apart there. Now a crop that CLIP is confident about never joins a track whose last confident crop was the other man. Position links the boxes, appearance vetoes the link.

Last one: on wide shots the referee's black trousers read as "dark trunks = Fury". Colour rules only get a vote now when there is a bare torso or CLIP gives at least some probability of a fighter. When Fury is in the dark behind the ropes and YOLO cannot see him at all, he simply gets no box, which beats a box on the wrong man.

The last change came from watching the demo clip: Usyk throws two hooks in a clinch and an uppercut, and the counter ignores all three. My punch signal was "wrist moving toward the opponent", which is a jab. A hook goes sideways and an uppercut goes up, so their forward component is zero or negative. The signal is now "fist leaving the body": the distance from the wrist to the centre of the torso, and how fast it grows. Jabs, hooks and uppercuts all do that; pulling the hand back does the opposite. It now counts the hooks.

Then the opposite problem: in the same clip it counted five Usyk punches where I saw two. Frame by frame: the wrist keypoint drops out for a few frames when the arm is hidden, YOLO parks it near the body, then it snaps back onto the extended arm. To a velocity detector that snap is a punch. One punch became three. Now no speed is computed across a frame where the wrist is not confidently seen, and a punch needs the opponent in frame at all. The price is a real uppercut that lands exactly on a dropout frame. I would rather miss that one than count phantoms.

When the swaps kept coming back, I stopped patching and had the two core files reviewed line by line by two separate agents with the data in hand. 25 findings, most of them measured on the real frames. The big one: my linker used position only and treated colour and CLIP as hard vetoes, and both vetoes were switched off exactly in clinches, where they were needed. Now the assignment is a joint per-frame optimisation with colour and CLIP as soft costs, and a chain remembers its colour and label as running averages so one bad sample cannot poison it. The review also caught that 16% of punches had the wrong jab/power type because I guessed the lead hand per frame instead of knowing the stance.

The last bug came from a friend watching the clip: "Fury barely throws and he is already at 5". Two of those five were his arm dropping to his side after a break. My normalisation switched between two scale estimates whenever the hips left the frame, and a 3x scale jump looks exactly like a fast punch. One scale per stretch, carried across the unseen frames, and "fist leaving the body" now excludes motion that is mostly downward. What still counts and should not: a hand on the opponent's neck in a clinch. Every geometric rule I tried for clinches removed as many real punches as grabs, so that stays in the error budget and in the write-up.

And the one that made the counter look blind in the corner sequence: Usyk lands six in three seconds, the counter saw three. Each miss had the same shape: the peak of the punch fell on a frame where the identity step had dropped him for one frame, or where the box around him ballooned with the extended arm and my cut detector read that as a camera cut and split the series. Fixed by bridging single-frame gaps and judging continuity on the shoulders, which do not move when the arm does. Four of six at the threshold I ship; the other two sit just under it, in the band where the detector is a coin flip.

So for the clip I stopped trusting the detector and verified it: every candidate in the 30 seconds, viewed frame by frame, plus the whole window as contact sheets to catch what the detector never proposed. 12 real punches, Usyk 11, Fury 1. The overlay in the clip draws those, not the raw output, and each point comes with a photo of the punch it was given for. The raw output finds 8 of the 12 and adds 3 that are not punches. That gap is the honest number for where this is.

Ground truth is external only: official scorecards, CompuBox totals (system: Fury 450 / Usyk 435 thrown, CompuBox: 496 / 407), and one round checked frame by frame: every candidate the detector produced in round 5 (196 of them at a low threshold) viewed as a 5-frame strip and marked. At the working threshold the precision is 0.79 on 235 verified candidates, and it recovers 41 of the 86 punches I verified. A lower threshold finds 53 but the extra ones are a coin flip, and in the demo they show up as Fury "punching" while he is getting hit. I kept the stricter one. The false ones are clinches, identity swaps on wide shots, and a few camera cuts that the cut detector missed. Nothing is labelled with the tracker's own output. That was the lesson from @Ammar's MMA version and I wanted to keep it from day one.
